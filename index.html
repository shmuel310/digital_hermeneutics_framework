From Knowledge Silos to Autonomous Swarm Orchestration

An agentic, multi-model infrastructure designed for academic biblical exegesis, verifiable knowledge provenance, and whole-canon continuous hermeneutical compounding.

Architectural Overview Operational Framework & Swarm Engine

The Digital Hermeneutics Framework (DHF) decouples complex academic research into two synergistic layers: an active Dynamic Execution Layer (comprising specialized expert councils and adversarial gates) and a permanent Static Precipitation Layer (a 6-tier ontological LLM Wiki spanning L0 raw transcripts to L5 canonical research gap registries).

This document synthesizes both the operational implementation (grounded in primary ancient texts via Sefaria and OpenGNT APIs) and the autonomous multi-agent swarm engine (an Antigravity-native, cron-driven upgrade system with atomic POSIX locks, deterministic floor scoring, and zero-loss archival rollback).

02 / Theoretical Grounding

The Epistemic Crisis in Biblical & Academic Scholarship

Contemporary humanistic research and biblical exegesis face an acute cognitive crisis. Over the past half-century, the exponential growth of critical literature including major commentary series, Dead Sea Scrolls corpora, papyrological databases, and sociological models has completely overwhelmed individual human working memory. This explosion produces two systemic failure modes:

Pathology A

Knowledge Silos

Insights extracted during intensive pericope exegesis remain locked in private reading notes, isolated document drafts, or transient mental memory. When scholars proceed to subsequent texts, cross-canonical links, verbal inclusios, and thematic trajectories dissipate.

Result: Zero compounding interest. Each new study restarts from scratch.
Pathology B

Frame-Lock

Researchers inherently operate within ingrained methodological and ideological traditions (historical-critical, rhetorical, social-scientific, or dogmatic). Cognitive bias cements an interpretive paradigm early in the inquiry, blinding the reader to competing linguistic or manuscript data.

Result: Exegetical fallacies, illegitimate totality transfer, and parallelomania.
"The Digital Hermeneutics Framework does not exist to replace scholarly discernment with automated text generation. It exists to force multi-perspective dialectics, eliminate cognitive blind spots, and anchor every theological deduction in verifiable ancient evidence."

03 / Implementation Architecture

Implementation Architecture: Two Hands, One Mind

To resolve the twin crises of Knowledge Silos and Frame-Lock, DHF implements a dual-layer architecture operating in continuous dialectical feedback:

The Dynamic Hands: Multi-Agent Council & Tooling

Rather than relying on monolithic generalist prompts, DHF orchestrates twelve specialized methodological agents held accountable by an adversarial Devil's Advocate auditor. Integrated via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), agents autonomously retrieve primary ancient languages, examine lexical semantics, evaluate ancient context, and query a curated private library of critical research databases.

The Permanent Brain: The 6-Tier Compounding Knowledge Graph

Scholarly breakthroughs cannot remain ephemeral chat logs. The system continually synthesizes debate outputs into an interconnected, file-based Markdown vault structured across six explicit ontological tiers (L0 through L5). Each file incorporates strict YAML provenance tracking to eliminate citation drift and maintain academic lineage.

04 / Knowledge Repository

The 6-Tier Compounding Knowledge Graph (L0 to L5)

Grounded in the foundational principle that humans curate sources and frame questions, while LLMs maintain, cross-link, and synthesize the knowledge base, DHF organizes research across a rigorous 6-tier ontological hierarchy. This structure prevents system entropy and allows research depth to compound over decades of scholarship.

Concept & Original Proposal

What is an LLM Wiki?

Read Karpathy’s LLM Wiki proposal

An LLM Wiki is a knowledge base that a language model actively helps maintain: it can create and revise Markdown notes, connect related ideas, and build a searchable web of knowledge as new material is researched. Rather than treating each chat as a disposable answer, the aim is to let understanding accumulate over time. DHF adapts this general idea into a tiered, source-traceable research repository; its six-level structure and scholarly controls are specific to this framework.

About the author: Andrej Karpathy is an AI researcher and engineer, co-founder of OpenAI and a former OpenAI researcher, and formerly director of AI at Tesla. The linked gist is his original proposal; the six-tier DHF implementation described here is an adaptation, not a claim that Karpathy authored this particular architecture.

05 / The Multi-Perspective Engine

The Disciplinary Council & Adversarial Gate (12+1 Voices)

The framework instantiates twelve domain-specific scholarly roles, each bounded by strict methodological prompt guidelines, and subjects them to an autonomous thirteenth agent: The Devil's Advocate.

Concept & Open-Source Reference

What is the LLM Council?

Explore LLM Council on GitHub

LLM Council is an open-source experiment by Andrej Karpathy that has multiple language models independently answer a prompt, compare their responses, and use a chairperson-style synthesis to produce a final answer. The core idea is to treat model diversity as a way to surface disagreements and improve the quality of an answer—not as a guarantee that the consensus is correct.

Who is Andrej Karpathy? Karpathy is an AI researcher and engineer, a co-founder of OpenAI who formerly worked there, and a former director of AI at Tesla. His experiments and explanations have helped make modern deep-learning and LLM techniques accessible to a broad technical audience.

07 / Autonomous Swarm Engine

The Swarm & Gold-Standard Engine

As the knowledge repository expanded across 750 or more canonical commentaries, human-dependent batch scripting reached its structural limits. The Multi-Agent Swarm & Gold-Standard Upgrade Engine transitions the framework from procedural scripting into an autonomous, self-auditing swarm ecosystem.

1. Surgical Gap-Fill

Never rewrites from scratch. Audits existing text, identifies deficient pillars, dispatches specialists to fill those exact gaps, and preserves 90% or more of validated original commentary.

2. POSIX File Lock Ledger

All workflow coordination persists in an atomic file-locked disk ledger. Immune to conversational context drift and session timeouts.

3. Modular File Limits

Enforces strict file boundaries: every section file and dossier remains strictly below 20,000 characters, completely eliminating stream timeouts.

10 / Quality Verification

Deterministic Floor Scorer & The Hard Floor Rule

A major hazard of autonomous LLM evaluation is grading inflation: generative models tend to award passing marks to generic prose. To guarantee academic rigor, DHF couples qualitative assessment with a deterministic regular-expression AST scanner.

The Deterministic Hard Floor Cap Rule

If an existing or newly generated commentary possesses a deterministic marker density below 0.50 for any given pillar (e.g., lacking original-language Greek/Hebrew token density, failing to name at least 3 of Hays's 7 criteria, or missing explicit verification and attribution tags), that pillar's score is hard-capped at 40% of its maximum weight, regardless of qualitative scoring.

PASS Criterion: (Total Score ≥ 85.0) & (∀ i in {1..6}, Pillar_i ≥ 0.50 * Weight_i) & (No Active Floor Caps)
Deterministic Pre-Flight Gate Invariants
1. Standardized Queries All specialist research queries must adhere strictly to academic English format.
2. Modular Size Cap Every section file and research dossier remains strictly below 20,000 characters to eliminate streaming timeouts.
3. Gate Clearance & Zero Veto Leakage The gate report must conclude with an explicit pass. Zero vetoed fallacy claims may appear in assembled drafts.
4. Non-Destructive Gap-Fill Assembled drafts must retain 90% or more of original character length and preserve formal academic register.

Architectural Terminology Reference
